Comfort, rest & sleep

Your instructor says the test will have sleep questions. Flagged

Circadian rhythm

Your ~24-hour internal clock. Syncs with light and temperature but runs even without cues.

Sleep–wake homeostasis

Sleep pressure. The more deprived you are, the deeper you sleep next time.

Sleep vs rest

Sleep = altered consciousness in cycles. Rest = awake but relaxed; still lowers stress and sharpens focus.

Sleep strengthens the immune system, restores energy, improves coping, clears brain toxins, and moves memory from short-term to long-term.

Stages & brain waves

Known mnemonic · brain waves from awake to deep sleep

"At night, BATS Drink Blood"

BBeta: awake, alert (13–30 Hz)
AAlpha: awake, relaxed, eyes closed (8–12 Hz)
TTheta: stage 1 (your module calls it low-amplitude, mixed-frequency)
SSpindles & K-complexes: stage 2
DDelta: stage 3, deepest
BBeta again: REM looks awake on EEG

The word "theta" isn't in your module, but the pattern is the same one it describes.

StageWhat's happeningShare of sleep (adults 20–35)
Stage 1 (NREM)Lightest sleep, 1–5 min, muscle twitching possible2–5%
Stage 2 (NREM)Harder to wake. HR and temp drop. Spindles and K-complexes help consolidate memory. Gets longer each cycle45–55%
Stage 3 (NREM)Deepest. Delta waves. Tissue, muscle, and bone repair, immune boost, lowest pulse and RR. Wake them and they're foggy 30–60 min25%
REM (stage 4)Dreaming. Irregular breathing, HR may rise, muscles atonic. Starts ~90 min in, first cycle ~10 min, up to 1 hr later20–25%

Memory hook · REM

Rapid Eyes, Muscles off

Your brain is busy dreaming, so your body is "paralyzed" to keep you from acting the dream out.

Adults

4–5 cycles a night. Recommended 7–9 hours.Slides content

Older adults

Sleep deprivation

Types

  • Total: a whole night lost
  • Partial: fewer hours
  • Chronic: partial that keeps going
  • Selective: a specific stage lost

Effects

Impaired judgment, slow reaction time, seizures, migraines, accidents, depression, stroke, obesity, poor glucose control in T2DM.

Sensory overload

Common in acute care. Dim lights, cut noise, offer earplugs and eye masks, cluster care.

Other disruptors: phone in bed (shorter, worse sleep; depression risk), night shift (higher CVD, obesity, diabetes risk), and menopause (sleep problems peak postmenopause).

Memory hook · night terrors vs nightmares

Terrors are early and erased. Nightmares are late and remembered.

Night terrors: NREM, first third of the night, no recall, mostly young kids. Nightmares: REM, second half of the night, you remember the dream.

  • Dark, quiet, cool room
  • Bedtime routine, short naps
  • Exercise, but not right before bed
  • Bed is for sleep and sex only; no TV
No coffee after 4 p.m. for a 10 p.m. bedtime

Quick check

You wake a client at 0200 and they're groggy for 45 minutes. Which stage were they in?

Stage 3. Deepest sleep, with mental cloudiness for 30–60 minutes after waking.

Which snack promotes sleep: cheese cubes, candy, or toast with peanut butter?

Toast with peanut butter. Carb plus protein. Cheese alone is protein only.

Most commonly prescribed class of sleep medication?

Nonbenzodiazepine hypnotics, like zolpidem.

Stress & coping

Stressors are neutral. Your appraisal decides whether they help or hurt.

The body under stress

Fight or flight (SAM system)

Norepinephrine, epinephrine, and dopamine in seconds. HR, BP, cardiac output, and glucose go up. Pupils and bronchi dilate.

Cortisol

Short-term: boosts some immune function. Chronic: suppresses immunity, slows healing, drives inflammation.

Memory hook · fight or flight

Everything you need to run goes up

Heart rate, BP, breathing, blood sugar, and pupil size all rise to help you fight or flee. Digestion slows down because you don't need it to escape.

The HPA axis

Hypothalamus releases CRH→ Pituitary releases ACTH→ Adrenals release cortisol

Top of the head to the top of the kidneys: Hypothalamus, Pituitary, Adrenals, in that order.

General adaptation syndrome

Memory hook

"A-R-E you stressed?"

Alarm, Resistance, Exhaustion. Happens with good or bad stress, physical or psychological.

Alarm Flagged

CNS aroused. Adrenal medulla releases epinephrine: fight or flight. HR, BP, glucose, O₂ intake, alertness up; pupils dilate.

Resistance

Body fights back. Parasympathetic system calms things down. Coping kicks in. Fatigue, irritability, poor focus.

Exhaustion

Resources used up. Illness, disease, and allostatic load (chronic HTN, depression, autoimmune disease).

Memory hook · ASD vs PTSD

ASD is Acute. PTSD Persists.

ASD: starts right after the trauma and lasts up to 1 month. Past 1 month, it's PTSD: flashbacks, nightmares, sweating, fast HR, exaggerated startle.

Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs): a score of 4 or more significantly raises the risk of chronic illness as an adult.

Appraisal & coping (TTSC)

Primary appraisal

"Is this a threat?" Threat, challenge, or irrelevant.

Secondary appraisal

"Can I handle it?" Checks resources: support, skills, past wins.

Reappraisal

Keeps reassessing and adjusts coping as things change.

Problem-focused

Adaptive vs maladaptive

Adaptive helps now and later. Maladaptive helps now, hurts later (smoking, drinking, withdrawal).Going for a walk vs. smoking after a hard day

Crisis

4 types of crisis

Developmental

Life transitions (puberty, retirement).

Situational

Outside events (job loss, disaster).

Existential

Meaning, identity, purpose.

Psychiatric emergency

Suicide attempt, psychosis.

Mental health emergency signs: acting on a suicide threat, hostility, throwing or breaking things, threatening harm.

Roberts' seven-stage model, in order:

  1. Assess, including suicide and homicide risk
  2. Build rapport
  3. Identify the problem and what triggered it
  4. Explore feelings
  5. Explore past coping and alternatives
  6. Carry out the action plan
  7. Follow up

Nursing care & self-care

First action

Assess. A mental status exam comes before goals or interventions.

Priority

Find out whether the client has thoughts of self-harm.

Perceived Stress Scale

Self-report of how unpredictable, uncontrollable, and overwhelming life has felt this past month.

Trauma-informed care

Past trauma shapes responses. Build safety and trust; don't retraumatize.

Exercise

Improves mood, energy, and sleep, lowers stress, releases endorphins.

Food

Complex carbs, oranges (vitamin C), omega-3s, black tea. Alcohol raises cortisol.

Relaxation

Mindfulness, guided imagery, progressive relaxation (tense and release, head to toe), biofeedback.

Moral distress

Your actions don't match what you believe is right. Use debriefings, ethics committees, peer support, and the ANA Code of Ethics.

Healthy Nurse, Healthy Nation

ANA program: quality of life, safety, physical activity, sleep, nutrition.

Diversity, equity & inclusion

Always ask. Don't assume.

Culture & bias

Culture (Leininger)

Learned, shared, and passed-down values, beliefs, norms, and lifeways that guide a group's thinking and decisions.

Cultural awareness

Being willing to understand differences between your culture and others'. Starts with checking your own bias.

Cultural competence

Appreciating, accepting, and respecting beliefs, plus open communication and feedback, blending emic and etic knowledge.

Explicit bias

Conscious. You know it and can admit it.A nurse says they'd rather not care for clients of a certain religion.

Implicit bias

Unconscious. You don't notice it.Spending less time with certain clients without realizing it.

Memory hook

eMic = Members. eTic = Theory.

Emic is the insider view from members of the culture. Etic is the outsider view from research, theories, and outside observation.

Cultural diversity happens within groups too, not just between them. Two clients from the same culture can have very different practices.

Health equity

Equality

Everyone gets the same box to stand on.

Equity

Everyone gets what they need to see over the fence.

Health disparities

Differences in outcomes between groups, often driven by social determinants of health like insurance or transportation.

Personal health literacy

Can this person find, process, and understand health info?

Organizational health literacy

Does the organization equitably help people find and use info and services?

Reading level

Write health materials at an 8th-grade level or lower.

CLAS standards: a blueprint for organizations to advance equity, improve quality, and eliminate disparities.

Vulnerable populations

Higher risk for poor outcomes because of barriers to social, economic, and environmental resources. Includes older adults, childrenSlides content, immigrants, marginalized ethnic groups, people in poverty, people with chronic illness or disabilities, veterans, and pregnant teens.

Demographic

Age, English proficiency, household type, population density, race and ethnicity, sex.

Health status

Chronic conditions, disabilities, insurance status.

Socioeconomic

Education, employment, household income, poverty.

Intersectionality

Several disparities at once, leading to more stress, lower self-esteem, and riskier behavior.

Population density

Nursing homes, shelters, and dorms spread infection faster.

LGBTQ+ youth

Higher risk of bullying, violence, depression, substance use, STIs, and suicide. Use chosen name and pronouns consistently.

Leininger's Sunrise Enabler

Five interrelated categories: the upper level (worldview), the rays of the sun (basic elements), the central core, the focus on individuals through institutions, and the three modes of care.

The rays: basic elements to assess

Technological (phone, internet access), religious and spiritual, kinship and social (support system, marital status), cultural values and lifeways, biological (personal or family illness history), political and legal, economic, educational.

Assessment & communication

Cultural health assessment

Start at the very first encounter. Name or gender may differ from records, so ask respectfully.

Language barrier

Use a certified medical interpreter. Never family or other staff: they can omit or alter info and break confidentiality. Add written instructions in the client's language.

Hearing loss

Face the client and reduce background noise. Don't shout, speed up, or write everything down.
